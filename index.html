PIFRA Registration: A First-Timer's Starter Guide

If you have just joined government service — or you have been serving for years but still collect your salary slip from the accounts office every month — this page walks you through PIFRA registration in the order you will actually meet it: what the system is, what to gather before you sit down, and where first attempts usually go wrong.

PIFRA stands for Project to Improve Financial Reporting and Auditing. Behind the long name sits a simple bargain: you register your personal email address against your payroll record once, and from then on your monthly salary slip arrives in your inbox on its own. No queue at the accounts office, no photocopies, and a growing archive of slips you can search whenever a bank, an embassy or your own tax return asks for proof of income. This starter guide keeps to the essentials; for the complete walkthrough with every service covered, the PIFRA Registration guide is the fuller reference.

Who actually needs to register

Registration is open to people whose pay runs through the government payroll system: federal and provincial government employees, pensioners who want their monthly pension slip by email, and Drawing and Disbursing Officers (DDOs) who deal with departmental reporting. If your salary is prepared by an Accountant General office or a district accounts office, your record almost certainly sits in the system already — registration simply connects your email to it.

Before you start: six things to have in front of you

Most failed registrations are not system failures. They are typing failures — one digit or one date that does not match the departmental record. Gather these six items first and copy them exactly as they appear in your service record:

  • Government Code — the short code for the government you serve under (see the table below).
  • Personnel (employee) number — issued by your department; it is not your CNIC number.
  • CNIC number — all 13 digits, typed without spaces or dashes.
  • Date of birth — as recorded in your service record, which is not always the date on your CNIC if a correction was never filed.
  • Mobile number — an active one, in case verification is needed.
  • A personal email address — this becomes the delivery address for every future salary slip, so use one you will still have in ten years, not an office inbox you may lose on transfer.

District government employees in Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa use their own district codes (DGP and DGN). If you are unsure which code covers your department, ask your DDO or accounts office before submitting — a wrong code points the form at the wrong payroll entirely.

The registration, step by step

  1. Open the official PIFRA salary slip registration page in your browser.
  2. Select your Government Code and enter your personnel number, CNIC and date of birth.
  3. Add your mobile number and review every field once, digit by digit.
  4. Enter your email address and type it a second time to confirm it.
  5. Submit. If the details match the official record, the email is linked to your payroll and slips begin arriving with the next monthly payroll cycle.

The form itself, field by field

Each field on the form is checked against a specific record, which is why a single wrong entry stops the whole process rather than producing a partial registration. Three mistakes that make first attempts fail

The date of birth mismatch. The commonest of all. Your CNIC may carry one date while your departmental service record carries another, often because of an old clerical entry. The form believes the service record. If your date is rejected, do not keep retrying variations — get the record corrected through your department first.

The CNIC typed from memory. Thirteen digits are easy to transpose. Type them from the card itself, and remember: no dashes, even though the card prints them.

"Email already registered." This message means the address is already linked to a payroll record — sometimes your own, from an earlier attempt you forgot about. Registering a second time will not fix it; the support office or your accounts office has to clear the existing link.

After you register: your first slip normally arrives with the next payroll run, not immediately. When it does not appear, check the spam or junk folder before assuming anything is broken — a first slip from a new sender is exactly what spam filters like to catch. Mark it "not spam" once and later slips land in the inbox.

A short word on safety

Your CNIC number and personnel number together identify your payroll record, so treat them the way you treat a bank PIN. Never post them publicly, never send them to unofficial pages or agents offering to "register you" for a fee, and only ever enter them on the official government portal. Registration itself is free.

Questions first-timers ask

Is PIFRA registration compulsory? No rule forces it, but without it there is no email delivery of salary slips — you are back to collecting paper copies from the accounts office.

Can pensioners register the same way? Yes. Pensioners register against their pension record and receive a monthly pension slip by email instead of a salary slip.

I transferred to another department. Must I register again? Your payroll record moves with you, but if your slips stop arriving after a transfer, tell your new accounts office — the email link sometimes needs to be refreshed against the updated record.

What if my employee record is "not found"? That almost always means a wrong personnel number or a record that has not been updated. Confirm the number with your DDO before trying again.
